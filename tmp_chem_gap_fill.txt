Answer key and review notes
Q Ans Spec Diff. Short rationale
1 B C17.1 2/5 Oxygen occupies 0.2095 × 200 = 41.9 cm³, so 200 - 41.9 = 158.1 cm³ remains.
2 C C17.1 2/5 The component with the lowest boiling point vaporises first: nitrogen, then argon, then
oxygen.
3 F C17.2 2/5 Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Greenhouse warming is associated with reduced loss
of infrared radiation, not mainly reflection of visible light.
4 A C17.3 3/5 Incomplete combustion can form CO. High engine temperatures can produce nitrogen
oxides from nitrogen and oxygen in air.
5 C C17.3 2/5 Sulfur in the fuel can form SO₂ on combustion; SO₂ contributes to acid rain and
environmental acidification.
6 E C17.4 1/5 Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Chlorination is for disinfection, not desalination.
7 B C15.1 1/5 A liquid has particles close together but in a disordered arrangement, able to move past
one another.
8 E C15.2 2/5 Melting changes particle arrangement and freedom of movement, not chemical identity;
particles in a liquid remain close together.
9 C C15.2 2/5 During a change of state, energy is used to overcome attractive forces and change
particle arrangement rather than raise the temperature.
10 B C15.2 2/5 Condensation changes gas to liquid: particles become closer and less free to move,
and energy is released to the surroundings.
11 A C16.1 1/5 These are the standard tests for H₂, O₂, CO₂ and Cl₂ respectively.
12 D C16.2,C16.4 2/5 A lilac flame indicates K⁺; a cream silver halide precipitate indicates Br⁻, so KBr fits both
tests.
13 A C16.2,C16.3 2/5 Green Fe(OH)₂ identifies Fe²⁺, while the acidified barium test identifies sulfate, giving
FeSO₄.
14 C C16.2,C16.4 2/5 A green flame identifies Cu²⁺ and a white silver halide precipitate identifies chloride, so
CuCl₂ is consistent.
15 B C16.5 2/5 The test confirms the presence of water, but it does not establish purity, pH or boiling
point.
16 A C14.3 3/5 For an overhead cable, low mass is important while good conductivity and corrosion
resistance are also needed. P best combines these properties.
17 B C14.3 2/5 The alloy has a much greater tensile strength but a smaller extension before breaking,
so it is stronger and less ductile.
18 H C14.5 2/5 All three are specified common properties of transition metals.
19 H C14.1,C14.4 2/5 All three are consistent with the reactivity/extraction model: highly reactive metals
require electrolysis, and metal extraction involves reduction.
20 A C13.1c,C13.3c2/5 Balancing atoms gives C₅H₁₀, an alkene, so it decolourises bromine water.
Q Ans Spec Diff. Short rationale
21 B C13.1d 2/5 Both have molecular formula C₄H₁₀ but different atom connectivity, so they are
structural isomers.
22 A C13.4b 4/5 A diol plus a dicarboxylic acid forms ester links, giving -O-(diol residue)-O-CO-(diacid
residue)-CO-.
23 B C13.4b,C13.4c3/5 A diamine and dicarboxylic acid form a polyamide with -CONH- links by condensation,
eliminating water.
24 B C4.2 2/5 0.25 mol O₂ contains 0.50 mol O atoms, so the number of atoms is 0.50 × 6.0 × 10²³ =
3.0 × 10²³.
25 D C4.9b 3/5 At 60 °C, 50 g water dissolves 40 g X. At 20 °C it can retain 15 g, so 25 g crystallises.
26 C C4.11 3/5 The predicted mass is 0.100 × 56.0 = 5.60 g. Percentage yield = 4.20/5.60 × 100 =
75%.
27 A C8.2,C8.3 2/5 Copper(II) sulfate dissolves in water but sand does not. Filtration separates sand;
crystallisation recovers the dissolved salt.
28 B C8.3 3/5 First remove the insoluble solid by filtration, then separate the two miscible liquids by
fractional distillation.
29 B C7.1 1/5 Argon is a noble gas and is very unreactive, so it can protect hot metal from reaction
with the surrounding air.
30 E C12.2,C12.6 3/5 The object being plated is the cathode; a silver anode can replenish Ag⁺. Direct current
is required so the electrode roles do not keep reversing.
