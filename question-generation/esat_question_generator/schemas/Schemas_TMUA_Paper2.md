## **R_8dd5ad2b. Disprove Universal Statements via Counterexamples**

**Core move:** Identify a specific case that violates the universal statement, thus proving it false.

**Seen in / context:**
- Questions involving "all", "every", or "for all" statements.
- Mathematical statements about the properties of numbers or functions.
- Logical reasoning problems requiring the validation of claims.
- Situations where a general rule is presented and needs to be tested.

**Possible wrong paths:**
- Assuming the statement is true and trying to prove it.
- Focusing on cases where the statement holds true, rather than seeking a counterexample.
- Misinterpreting the universal quantifier and its scope.
- Getting bogged down in complex calculations without finding a simple counterexample.

**Notes for generation:**
- Create statements that seem plausible but have easily identifiable counterexamples.
- Use mathematical concepts where specific values can easily break the general rule.
- Vary the complexity of the statement to test different levels of understanding.
- From TMUA Paper 2 (Mathematical Reasoning)

**Exemplar questions:**
- `TMUA_Paper2_2017_Q5`: The statement claims that for all positive integers n, n^2 + 1 is prime. The counterexample n=1 shows that 1^2 + 1 = 2, which is prime. However, n=7 gives 7^2 + 1 = 50, which is not prime. Therefore, the statement is false.
