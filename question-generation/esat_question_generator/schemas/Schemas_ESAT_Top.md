## **M_5e59fdaf. Numerical Sequence with Increasing Differences**

**Core move:** Identify a pattern in a numerical sequence where the difference between consecutive terms increases, and then extrapolate to find the next term.

**Seen in / context:**
- Sequences of numbers where the differences between consecutive terms are not constant.
- Problems requiring pattern recognition and extrapolation.
- Questions involving arithmetic or quadratic sequences.

**Possible wrong paths:**
- Assuming a constant difference between terms.
- Miscalculating the differences between terms.
- Incorrectly extrapolating the pattern.

**Notes for generation:**
- The differences between terms should increase in a predictable way (e.g., linearly, quadratically).
- The sequence should have enough terms to establish a clear pattern.
- Avoid sequences with overly complex or ambiguous patterns.

**Exemplar questions:**
- `NSAA_Section1_2020_Q9`: The differences between consecutive terms increase linearly, allowing for extrapolation.
- `NSAA_Section1_2020_Q10`: The differences between consecutive terms increase quadratically, allowing for extrapolation.
- `NSAA_Section1_2020_Q11`: The differences between consecutive terms increase exponentially, allowing for extrapolation.

## **M_1af88dc1. Elimination by Contradiction with Given Constraints**

**Core move:** Eliminate answer choices that violate stated constraints or established mathematical/scientific principles to identify the correct solution.

**Seen in / context:**
- Problems with specific limitations on variable values (e.g., integer solutions only).
- Questions involving physical laws or mathematical theorems that must be obeyed.
- Scenarios where certain outcomes are logically impossible given the premises.
- Problems where units or dimensions must be consistent.

**Possible wrong paths:**
- Failing to fully consider all given constraints.
- Making assumptions that contradict the problem statement.
- Selecting an answer that is mathematically or physically impossible.
- Overlooking subtle implications of the constraints.

**Notes for generation:**
- Ensure the constraints are clearly stated and unambiguous.
- Design incorrect answer choices that violate at least one constraint.
- The correct answer should satisfy all constraints and principles.

**Exemplar questions:**
- `NSAA_Section1_2022_Q9`: Eliminate options that do not satisfy the given equation.
- `NSAA_Section1_2022_Q0`: Eliminate options that contradict the information given in the passage.
- `Generic_Maths_Problem_1`: Eliminate solutions that result in undefined operations (e.g., division by zero).

## **M_ba325a79. T Exam Structure: Parts and Page Numbers**

**Core move:** The exam is divided into distinct parts, each covering a specific subject, and a table of contents lists the starting page number for each part.

**Seen in / context:**
- An exam paper is divided into sections (e.g., Maths, Physics, etc.).
- A table of contents or similar index provides page numbers for each section.
- Questions require finding the correct section based on the subject matter.

**Possible wrong paths:**
- Assuming all questions are in sequential order by topic.
- Overlooking the table of contents or index provided.
- Misinterpreting the subject matter of a question and looking in the wrong section.

**Notes for generation:**
- Create exam structures with clearly defined sections.
- Include a table of contents or similar guide with page numbers.
- Questions should be easily categorized into one of the defined sections.

**Exemplar questions:**
- `NSAA_Section1_2021_Q3`: This question requires identifying the subject matter (area) and then using the table of contents to find the correct section of the exam.
- `PAT_2018_Q1`: The question requires identifying the relevant section based on the physics topic and using the provided structure to locate it.
- `ENGAA_2023_Q1`: The question requires identifying the relevant section based on the engineering topic and using the provided structure to locate it.
